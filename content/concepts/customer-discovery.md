# Customer Discovery

Customer discovery is the disciplined practice of learning from real customer behavior before committing to a solution. It is not just asking people what they think; it is reconstructing their actual story: what triggered them, what they tried, which existing alternatives they used, what made them consider change, and what friction, constraints, and desired outcomes shaped their decisions.

In *Running Lean, 3rd Edition*, customer discovery is not a one-off research phase. It is part of the broader [[concepts/continuous-innovation]] system, where teams repeatedly model, prioritize, test, review, and update their business model. The preface highlights a revised problem discovery interview script for uncovering real customer problems worth solving [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-Preface-to-the-Third-Edition]], pp. 6-10]. The introduction argues that founders should start with problems, not solutions, and that discovery helps them avoid the [[concepts/build-trap]] and move toward [[concepts/traction]] [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-Introduction]], pp. 11-49]. Part I embeds discovery into business model design by using the [[concepts/lean-canvas]] to surface and stress test customer assumptions [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-I-Design]], pp. 50-58; Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-1-Deconstruct-Your-Idea-on-a-Lean-Canvas]], pp. 59-98].

Chapter 8 adds the most detailed discovery method in the book: problem discovery interviews, timeline reconstruction, the [[concepts/customer-forces]] canvas, and jobs-based customer segmentation [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-8-Understand-Your-Customers-Better-Than-They-Do]], pp. 300-345]. Chapter 9 connects discovery to solution design and MVP packaging [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-9-Design-Your-Solution-to-Cause-a-Switch]], pp. 346-367]. Chapter 10 uses the same discovery work to assemble a compelling mafia offer [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-10-Deliver-a-Mafia-Offer-Your-Customers-Cannot-Refuse]], pp. 368-401]. Chapter 11 shows that discovery also informs 90-day cycle reviews, updated pitches, and next-cycle decisions [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-11-Run-a-90-Day-Cycle-Review]], pp. 402-423]. Chapter 12 extends this into launch readiness by emphasizing staged rollout, early-access customers, continuous learning, and customer factory metrics after the MVP is ready [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-12-Get-Ready-to-Launch]], pp. 424-439].

Chapter 14 extends the same logic into growth: once a product is live, the team must keep studying real behavior to find a sustainable growth rocket. Discovery does not stop at validation; it continues into retention, referral, content, and other feedback loops that can power scalable acquisition [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-14-Find-Your-Growth-Rocket]], pp. 467-487]. The epilogue reinforces this as part of the bootstart mindset: entrepreneurs should act, learn, and refine rather than wait for permission or a perfect plan [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-15-Epilogue]], pp. 488-494].

> [!note]
> In Maurya's framework, customer discovery is not the same as asking whether people like your idea. It is a disciplined way to learn what people are already trying to do, what breaks, what they do next, and what would make them switch.

## What customer discovery does

Customer discovery helps teams:

- identify painful, high-priority problems
- understand a customer's current workflow and existing alternatives
- uncover switching triggers and expectation violations
- detect friction, workarounds, and unmet needs
- learn the customer's language and mental models
- avoid building around false assumptions
- refine customer segments and early adopters
- populate and improve a [[concepts/lean-canvas]]
- group recurring stories into jobs-based customer segments
- determine what kind of MVP packaging can cause a switch
- shape a stronger offer before building
- support 90-day cycle review decisions with evidence
- keep learning after launch through batches, check-ins, and rollout feedback
- identify retention, referral, and content behaviors that can become growth loops
- move from "I have an idea" to "I have evidence of a meaningful problem"

Discovery is the front end of a learning system. It gives teams the raw material they need to design solutions, validate demand, and make evidence-based decisions instead of relying on optimism alone.

## Why it matters

Maurya argues that early-stage product work happens under extreme uncertainty, so teams need a disciplined way to stress test the problem side of the business model before investing in solutions [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-Preface-to-the-Third-Edition]], pp. 6-10]. Customers also have many choices, which means weak products often do not generate useful learning; they simply lose attention. Discovery is therefore not just research. It is the mechanism for finding problems strong enough to support a viable business model [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-Introduction]], pp. 11-49].

The third edition places discovery inside a broader Continuous Innovation system. In Part I, the Lean Canvas is used to deconstruct the idea, then desirability is stress tested before the team moves on to viability and feasibility. That means discovery is part of the design process itself, not something done after the "real work" begins [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-I-Design]], pp. 50-58]. Chapter 1 reinforces the point by showing that the first pass should be a one-page model of the business, intentionally incomplete so it can be refined through customer learning [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-1-Deconstruct-Your-Idea-on-a-Lean-Canvas]], pp. 59-98].

Chapter 8 shows that discovery becomes especially powerful when you stop asking customers to list abstract problems and instead reconstruct their story around actual behavior. By tracing triggering events, existing alternatives, desired outcomes, and friction, you can uncover the real job-to-be-done and classify recurring patterns into jobs-based segments [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-8-Understand-Your-Customers-Better-Than-They-Do]], pp. 300-345]. Chapter 9 then uses those insights to choose the smallest solution that can cause a switch [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-9-Design-Your-Solution-to-Cause-a-Switch]], pp. 346-367]. Chapter 10 turns them into a story-driven offer, Chapter 11 uses them again to evaluate what happened and decide whether to persevere, pivot, or pause, and Chapter 12 keeps the loop running during launch with staged rollout and early-access feedback [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-10-Deliver-a-Mafia-Offer-Your-Customers-Cannot-Refuse]], pp. 368-401; Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-11-Run-a-90-Day-Cycle-Review]], pp. 402-423; Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-12-Get-Ready-to-Launch]], pp. 424-439].

Chapter 14 adds another important layer: discovery is also how you uncover sustainable growth. Once teams start seeing repeatable behavior, they can study what existing customers do that can fuel future acquisition through revenue, content/data, or referrals [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-14-Find-Your-Growth-Rocket]], pp. 467-487]. The epilogue makes this explicit as part of the bootstart manifesto: entrepreneurs should act now, remove fear of failure, and use disciplined learning to improve their odds [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-15-Epilogue]], pp. 488-494].

Without discovery, teams risk:

- solving the wrong problem
- mistaking politeness for demand
- confusing interest with commitment
- treating assumptions as evidence
- building an MVP before understanding demand
- targeting the wrong segment or switching trigger
- mixing multiple business model stories into one canvas
- designing a solution that does not cause a switch
- pitching before the customer story is clear
- failing to learn from cycle reviews and updating the model too slowly
- launching too broadly before value delivery is repeatable
- missing the behavioral signals that could later power a growth loop

## Core principles

### 1) Start with the problem, not the solution
Discovery should reveal what customers are already trying to do, what frustrates them, and what alternatives they use today. Customers care more about their problems and outcomes than your solution [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-Introduction]], pp. 11-49]. Chapter 1 adds that a Lean Canvas should begin by deconstructing the big idea into specific assumptions, especially customer segments and problems, because those are the best starting points for discovery [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-1-Deconstruct-Your-Idea-on-a-Lean-Canvas]], pp. 59-98].

### 2) Look for evidence, not opinions
Good discovery searches for concrete examples, past behavior, and recurring pain points. People may like an idea in the abstract, but actual behavior is a stronger signal than enthusiasm.

### 3) Validate pain and priority
A customer problem is worth solving only if it is important enough to trigger action. Discovery should determine whether the issue is severe, frequent, urgent, and costly enough to motivate change.

### 4) Use discovery to shape the offer
Maurya's process is not "discover problem, then build MVP." It is "discover problem, define solution, craft offer, then build solution." Discovery feeds the offer before the product is fully built [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-Introduction]], pp. 11-49]. Chapter 9 makes this concrete by showing how the discovered struggle determines what kind of MVP packaging is appropriate [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-9-Design-Your-Solution-to-Cause-a-Switch]], pp. 346-367]. Chapter 10 extends that logic into a mafia offer, where the story, demo, price, and CTA are all assembled from discovery insights [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-10-Deliver-a-Mafia-Offer-Your-Customers-Cannot-Refuse]], pp. 368-401].

### 5) Discovery is ongoing, not one-time
Chapter 11 shows discovery continuing into 90-day cycle reviews, where updated canvases and traction roadmaps are compared against actual results. Chapter 12 extends that principle into launch, where early-access feedback, pilot management, and company-wide dashboards keep learning alive after the MVP ships [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-11-Run-a-90-Day-Cycle-Review]], pp. 402-423; Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-12-Get-Ready-to-Launch]], pp. 424-439]. The epilogue reinforces this as a long-term habit of action, iteration, and learning [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-15-Epilogue]], pp. 488-494].

### 6) Anchor on actual behavior
Maurya's discovery method centers on reconstructing a real timeline: trigger, consideration, selection, activation, retention, and next summit. That structure is designed to uncover what people actually did, not what they say they might do [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-8-Understand-Your-Customers-Better-Than-They-Do]], pp. 300-345].

### 7) Use customer forces to interpret change
Discovery is easier to make sense of when organized by push, pull, inertia, and friction. That lets teams see why a customer switched, what kept them with the old way, and what made the new way attractive.

### 8) Turn stories into segments
Recurring stories become jobs-based customer segments. This is more useful than demographic personas because it groups people by similar triggers, alternatives, outcomes, and switching behavior.

### 9) Discovery informs review and next-cycle decisions
Customer discovery does not end when a campaign starts. The insights it produces are revisited during 90-day cycle reviews, where teams decide whether to persevere, pivot, or pause based on what they learned [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-11-Run-a-90-Day-Cycle-Review]], pp. 402-423].

### 10) Discovery continues into launch and growth
Once early customers are lined up, discovery still matters. The launch phase in Chapter 12 emphasizes soft launches to early adopters, staged rollout in batches, and continuous measurement of customer factory metrics so the team keeps learning after the MVP ships [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-12-Get-Ready-to-Launch]], pp. 424-439]. Chapter 14 extends this again: the same behavioral learning helps identify content, data, or referral patterns that can become a sustainable growth loop [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-14-Find-Your-Growth-Rocket]], pp. 467-487].

## How *Running Lean* frames customer discovery

The book treats customer discovery as the first filter in a staged process:

1. Deconstruct your idea on a [[concepts/lean-canvas]]
2. Stress test desirability through customer discovery
3. Stress test viability
4. Stress test feasibility
5. Communicate the idea clearly and concisely

That makes discovery the first major step in turning a vague Plan A into a business model that can survive contact with reality [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-I-Design]], pp. 50-58]. Chapter 1 gives the practical start: sketch the model quickly, then refine assumptions as discovery reveals which beliefs matter most [Source: [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-1-Deconstruct-Your-Idea-on-a-Lean-Canvas]], pp. 59-98]. ## Discovery workflow in practice

A practical discovery workflow in Maurya's framework looks like this:

1. Define the customer segment broadly enough to see the market
2. Identify likely early adopters and their switching triggers
3. Reconstruct a real story around a chosen existing alternative
4. Map push, pull, inertia, and friction with the [[concepts/customer-forces]] canvas
5. Group similar stories into jobs-based segments
6. Decide whether the problem is big enough to warrant a switch
7. Use the learning to shape solution design, the MVP, the offer, and the next-cycle review
8. Keep validating after launch through batch rollout and customer feedback
9. Watch for retention, referral, or content behaviors that can become a growth loop

This workflow is designed to uncover not just what customers say, but what they actually experience as they move through change.

## Common failure modes

- asking leading questions that steer customers toward your idea
- collecting compliments instead of evidence
- interviewing people who are too far removed from the problem
- focusing on feature requests before understanding the underlying pain
- treating one conversation as enough to confirm a market
- jumping to MVP-building before problem/solution fit
- using metrics without talking to customers and missing the "why"
- mixing multiple business model stories into one canvas
- starting with the solution and then searching for a matching problem
- mistaking surveys for discovery when real learning requires conversation
- failing to update the model during 90-day reviews
- launching too broadly before value delivery is repeatable
- missing the behavioral signals that could later power a growth loop

## Deep Study

> [!tip]- ELI5
> Customer discovery means learning what real people already do, what hurts, and what makes them change before you build.

> [!tip]- Real-World Analogy
> It's like watching how people use a broken kitchen tool before inventing a new one: see the mess, the workaround, and the moment they want something better.

> [!warning]- Common Misconceptions
> Myth: Discovery is asking people if they like your idea. Reality: It's learning their real behavior, pains, and triggers.
> Myth: Surveys are enough. Reality: Surveys can confirm ideas, but conversations reveal the story and the hidden why.
> Myth: Discovery ends when the MVP ships. Reality: You keep learning during launch, retention, and growth.
> Myth: Customer discovery is separate from strategy. Reality: It shapes the Lean Canvas, MVP, offer, review cycle, and growth loops.
> Myth: People switch because a solution is slightly better. Reality: Switching usually happens when the old way breaks, becomes too painful, or a better promise is compelling enough.

> [!question]- Check Your Understanding
> **Q:** Why does discovery start with behavior instead of opinions?

Discovery is embedded in the design phase as the first step in Lean Canvas-driven business model design and desirability testing. This perspective emphasizes that customer discovery is not separate from product strategy; it is the mechanism for refining the business model before deeper validation.

### [[summaries/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-1-Deconstruct-Your-Idea-on-a-Lean-Canvas]] - Chapter 1: Deconstruct Your Idea on a Lean Canvas (pages 59-98)
Discovery begins with deconstructing the idea into a Lean Canvas, sketching assumptions quickly, and focusing on customer segments, problems, early adopters, and alternatives. This chapter frames customer discovery as the input to disciplined model refinement, not just a standalone interview practice.

Maurya deepens discovery into a structured interviewing practice. Discovery transitions directly into solution design. Once the primary struggle is understood, the team can choose the smallest solution that causes a switch and package it as a concierge MVP, Wizard-of-Oz MVP, or another lightweight validation form.

Discovery becomes the foundation for the customer story pitch and mafia offer. The chapter shows that deep customer understanding makes the offer more compelling, the demo more credible, and the call to action more effective.

Discovery extends into review and accountability. Updated customer learnings, canvases, and traction roadmaps are used to assess progress, decide whether to persevere, pivot, or pause, and define the next cycle's priorities.

Discovery continues into launch readiness. Maurya emphasizes soft launches to early adopters, staged rollout in batches, and continuous measurement of customer factory metrics so teams keep learning after the MVP ships.

Discovery evolves into growth analysis. Maurya argues that once behavior is repeatable, teams should study which customer actions can become sustainable growth loops powered by revenue, content/data, or referrals.

> [!tip]- ELI5
> Customer discovery means talking to real people and watching what they do before building a solution.

> [!tip]- Real-World Analogy
> It's like checking where the leaks are in a boat before deciding where to patch it.

> [!warning]- Common Misconceptions
> Myth: Customer discovery is just asking people if they like the idea. Reality: It is about learning their real behavior, pain, and triggers.
> Myth: Surveys are enough. Reality: Surveys can validate known hypotheses, but interviews uncover the deeper story.
> Myth: Discovery ends when the MVP is built. Reality: Discovery continues through launch, retention, and growth.
> Myth: Discovery is separate from strategy. Reality: It shapes the Lean Canvas, offer, MVP, review cycle, and growth loops.
