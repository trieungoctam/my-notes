# Now-Next-Later Rollout Plan

The **Now-Next-Later Rollout Plan** is a staged strategy for deciding what to do under uncertainty. It helps a team translate long-term ambition into a sequence of actions that matches its current risks, current bottlenecks, and current stage of learning.

In *Running Lean*, now-next-later sits inside the [[concepts/traction-roadmap]] and expresses the [[concepts/right-action-right-time]] mindset. It is both a communication tool and an operating tool: first to explain how the business is progressing, and then to decide what experiments, campaigns, MVP choices, launch steps, and growth bets should happen in the current cycle.

> [!note]
> In the 90-day review loop, now-next-later also becomes part of the evidence-based decision cycle: what happened this cycle determines whether the team should persevere, pivot, or pause, and what the next cycle should focus on.

## Definition

A now-next-later rollout plan is a **sequenced learning-and-execution strategy** that maps a startup's progress across three time horizons:

- **Now**: the immediate actions needed to de-risk the earliest assumptions
- **Next**: the intermediate actions that become possible after the first stage is validated
- **Later**: the broader future direction once the business has more proof, repeatability, and traction

The key principle is that each stage should address the **riskiest assumptions** for that phase, not the easiest tasks or the most attractive long-term ideas.

## How It Works in *Running Lean*

Maurya uses the now-next-later plan to make the [[concepts/traction-roadmap]] actionable. After estimating a three-year [[concepts/minimum-success-criteria|minimum success criteria]] and modeling a growth trajectory, the founder realizes that the full vision cannot be tackled all at once. The roadmap is therefore broken into stages:

- **Now** corresponds to **problem/solution fit**
- **Next** corresponds to **product/market fit**
- **Later** corresponds to **scale**

This framing helps founders avoid [[concepts/premature-optimization]]-for example, trying to build for scale before validating demand.

The book also shows that the now-next-later plan becomes sharper when it is connected to the current bottleneck in the [[concepts/customer-factory]]. Instead of spreading attention across all risks, the team focuses on the limiting constraint first, then revises the plan as the constraint shifts.

Later in the book, the same logic shows up in a more operational form: build the MVP fast, prepare for launch, then launch in small batches while continuing to keep the customer factory running. That makes now-next-later not just a roadmap metaphor, but a launch discipline too.

In the growth chapters, the plan expands again: once retention and repeatability are visible, the team starts allocating some attention to a primary [[concepts/growth-rocket]]. In that sense, now-next-later also becomes a way to separate **validation work now** from **scalable growth work later**.

> [!important]
> The epilogue reinforces the same discipline in manifesto form: start small, learn fast, avoid premature optimization, and sequence work by risk rather than by fantasy.

## Core Idea

The now-next-later plan exists to answer a simple question:

> What should we be doing right now, given what we know and what is riskiest?

Instead of treating a product roadmap as a fixed list of features, the plan treats startup execution as a learning sequence. Each stage has its own goal, evidence threshold, and level of specificity.

It is especially useful when a team already has ambition but lacks certainty. The plan keeps long-term vision intact while forcing short-term discipline.

## Stage Breakdown

### Now
The **now** stage is the most concrete. It focuses on the smallest actions needed to validate the earliest customer and market assumptions.

In the book, this usually means:

- narrowing the idea to a specific early adopter group
- finding a sharp [[concepts/unique-value-proposition]]
- testing whether customers care enough to commit
- using demo/sell-before-build approaches when possible
- identifying the current bottleneck in the customer factory and attacking it directly
- breaking the work into experiment-sized campaigns inside a 90-day cycle
- learning enough about customers to uncover their switching triggers, existing alternatives, and desired outcomes

This stage is about [[concepts/problem-first-thinking]] and achieving [[concepts/problem-solution-fit]]. In the validation chapters, "now" often means selecting one experiment or campaign that can most quickly prove or disprove the current hypothesis.

Chapter 9 tightens this further: the now stage should produce the **smallest solution that causes a switch**. That means the first rollout is not the full vision, but the minimum viable product or offer that can create real movement from an existing alternative to a better outcome.

### Next
The **next** stage is less concrete because it depends on what the now stage teaches. Here, the team begins building and improving the solution with the goal of repeatable traction.

This stage often involves:

- building an MVP
- improving activation and retention
- refining the offer
- using customer feedback loops
- turning a successful experiment into a larger 90-day campaign
- moving from modeling and prioritizing into sustained testing
- incorporating customer-discovery insights into the evolving solution

In Chapter 8, Maurya shows that the quality of the now stage depends heavily on how well the team understands customers. Once interview patterns reveal how customers currently use existing alternatives and what switching trigger moves them, the team can refine the next stage with far better information.

This is the bridge toward [[concepts/product-market-fit]]. In the validation sections, "next" is also what becomes possible once a current bottleneck has been reduced and the team has enough evidence to select the next constraint.

### Later
The **later** stage is the least specific because it belongs to a future in which the model is already working. At this point the focus shifts toward scale and growth:

- growth experiments
- channel optimization
- expansion to broader customer segments
- infrastructure and operational improvements
- choosing a primary [[concepts/growth-rocket]]

In the book, "later" is not ignored; it is deferred until the right earlier risks are solved. The point is to avoid making scale decisions before the business has earned them.

> [!tip]
> Now-next-later is not a promise that later work is unimportant. It is a discipline for not confusing future ambition with current priority.

## Why It Matters

The now-next-later rollout plan is important because early-stage founders often do the opposite: they plan too far ahead, overbuild, or optimize for growth before knowing whether anyone wants the product.

This concept helps teams:

- stay focused on the current bottleneck
- sequence work by risk
- avoid premature optimization
- keep long-term vision intact without becoming hostage to it
- communicate progress clearly to advisors, investors, and team members
- translate a 90-day cycle into concrete execution choices
- turn a traction roadmap into a practical operating rhythm
- connect customer discovery findings to the next stage of execution
- carry review outcomes forward into the next 90-day cycle
- launch in small batches without losing learning velocity
- separate validation work from growth-rocket work

It is a practical expression of [[concepts/continuous-innovation-framework]] and [[concepts/right-action-right-time]].

## Practical Use

To use a now-next-later plan, start with the biggest question at the earliest stage. Ask:

1. What is the riskiest assumption right now?
2. What evidence would prove or disprove it?
3. What is the smallest step that can generate that evidence?
4. What becomes the next risk after that?
5. What future ambitions should wait until later?

In practice, this means the **now** plan should be the most detailed, the **next** plan moderately detailed, and the **later** plan broad and directional.

## Deep Study

> [!tip]- ELI5
> Now-next-later means do the riskiest important thing now, prepare the follow-up next, and save the bigger future work for later.

> [!tip]- Real-World Analogy
> It's like fixing a house: stop the leak now, patch the damage next, and plan the remodel later.

> [!warning]- Common Misconceptions
> Myth: It is just a calendar. Reality: It is a risk-based learning plan.
> Myth: Later means never. Reality: Later means after the earlier uncertainty is reduced.
> Myth: Every stage should be equally detailed. Reality: Now should be concrete; next and later should stay progressively fuzzier.
> Myth: It is only for investor pitches. Reality: It also guides experiments, customer discovery, execution, and growth planning.
> Myth: It replaces strategy. Reality: It operationalizes strategy under uncertainty.
> Myth: It is the same as a product roadmap. Reality: It is a sequencing tool for risks, learning, and staged rollout.

> [!question]- Check Your Understanding
> **Q:** What problem does now-next-later solve?
> **A:** It prevents teams from trying to do everything at once when the riskiest assumptions should be tackled first.
>
> **Q:** How does it relate to the traction roadmap?
> **A:** The traction roadmap shows the milestone path, and now-next-later turns it into stage-specific actions.
>
> **Q:** Why is the now stage the most concrete?
> **A:** Because the earliest risks need immediate actions and fast evidence.
>
> **Q:** What should the next stage do?
> **A:** It should build on what the now stage learned and move the business closer to repeatable traction.
>
> **Q:** What is the main mistake this concept helps avoid?
> **A:** Premature optimization-working on scale or polish before proving demand.
>
> **Q:** How does Chapter 9 sharpen the concept?
> **A:** It shows that the now stage should deliver the smallest solution that can cause a switch, not the full product vision.
>
> **Q:** What changes after a 90-day review?
> **A:** The team updates its understanding of the current constraint and uses that evidence to choose the next now-next-later plan.

> [!info]- Why It Matters
> Now-next-later keeps teams from overplanning the future and under-solving the present. It preserves long-term ambition while forcing short-term discipline, making it easier to validate a business model, choose experiments, and explain the plan to investors, advisors, and teammates. In the 90-day operating rhythm, it turns a roadmap into a sequence of concrete bets and makes review decisions evidence-based. It also helps with early launch: ship small, learn fast, keep the customer factory running, and reserve later-stage attention for scalable growth rockets.
