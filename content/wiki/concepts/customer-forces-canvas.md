# Customer Forces Canvas

The **Customer Forces Canvas** is a structured interview synthesis tool for understanding why customers switch from an existing solution to a new one. It maps the behavioral forces shaping adoption and use: **push**, **pull**, **inertia**, and **friction**. In [[concepts/jobs-to-be-done]] terms, it helps reconstruct the customer's job, the trigger that activated it, the existing alternative they chose, and the choice process that led to a switch.

It is a key artifact in the discovery process described in [[summary/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-12-Get-Ready-to-Launch]] and is reinforced in [[summary/Running Lean, 3rd Edition  Iterate from Plan A to a Plan That Works Ash Maurya z-library.sk, 1lib.sk, z-lib.sk-15-Epilogue]]. The canvas turns raw interview notes into evidence about whether a customer problem is painful enough to justify a new solution.

## What the Canvas Captures

The canvas organizes a customer story into a set of causal fields:

- **Triggering event(s)** - what changed and started the journey
- **Switching trigger** - the event that caused the customer to consider changing
- **Desired outcome** - what the customer was trying to achieve
- **What's at stake** - what happens if they do nothing
- **Consideration set** - the alternatives they compared
- **Chosen solution** - the option they hired
- **Unique value proposition** - the promised outcome that pulled them in
- **Channel/price** - how they found it and what it cost
- **Existing solution / habits** - the status quo they were already using
- **Anxieties / usability issues** - the resistance encountered during adoption and use
- **Actual outcome / next summit** - what happened after adoption and what comes next

The point is not just to record opinions. The canvas is meant to capture the **timeline of change** and the customer's real behavior before, during, and after a switch.

## The Four Forces

The canvas is built around four behavioral forces:

### Push
Push is the motivation for change. It comes from a triggering event, unmet need, or dissatisfaction with the current outcome.

### Pull
Pull is the attraction of the new solution. It includes the promise of a better outcome, the value proposition, the channel, and the price.

### Inertia
Inertia is the comfort of the status quo. Customers often keep doing what they already do unless a switching trigger makes the old way feel inadequate.

### Friction
Friction is resistance to adopting the new solution. It includes anxiety, uncertainty, and usability issues that slow or block switching.

These forces matter because customers do not simply choose the objectively best solution. Adoption is emotional, contextual, and path-dependent.

> [!note]
> The canvas is not a static persona worksheet. It is a behavioral model of how a customer moves from one solution to another.

## How It Is Used

Maurya recommends using the Customer Forces Canvas during **problem discovery interviews**. The process is:

1. Start with a **broad-match** set of interviews to learn how people currently get the job done.
2. Reconstruct the timeline from first thought to switching trigger to current use.
3. Fill out the canvas immediately after each interview.
4. Group canvases into recurring **jobs-based customer segments**.
5. Use the patterns to decide whether there is a big enough problem worth solving.

This approach avoids the common mistake of asking people directly, "What problems do you have?" Instead, it uncovers problems by studying how customers use or abandon [[concepts/lean-canvas|existing alternatives]].

The interview flow is usually chronological:
- anchor on the last time the job was done or the existing solution was hired
- work backward to the first thought and switching trigger
- work forward through acquisition, activation, retention, and the next summit

That timeline-based method makes the canvas especially useful for understanding changes in behavior, not just stated preferences.

## Why the Canvas Is Powerful

The Customer Forces Canvas helps teams determine:

- whether a real switching opportunity exists,
- whether the problem is painful enough to justify a new product,
- which customer segment is most motivated to switch,
- and what promise might cause adoption.

It also helps prevent [[concepts/innovator-s-bias|Innovator's Bias]] by forcing teams to focus on the customer's story instead of their preferred solution.

The source material expands this by showing that many markets contain only a few recurring customer journey patterns. Once you have enough interviews, patterns usually settle into three to five common story clusters.

## Jobs-Based Segmentation

A major contribution of the canvas is that it enables **jobs-based customer segments**. Rather than segmenting only by demographics, the canvas groups people by shared:
- triggering events
- desired outcomes
- existing alternatives
- switching triggers

That is often more predictive of behavior than age, title, or geography.

In practice, this means a team may discover that several very different people are actually part of the same segment because they are all trying to solve the same job in the same way.

## Key Insights from the Sources

- Most customers cannot accurately state their deepest problems directly.
- The best discovery questions focus on **existing alternatives**, not abstract problems.
- Good segments are based on **shared triggers, outcomes, and alternatives**, not just demographics.
- The canvas is most powerful when filled out from a chronological customer story.
- Completed canvases often reveal 3-5 recurring journey patterns in a market.
- The method works best when used with 10-30 interviews across broad-match and narrow-match batches.
